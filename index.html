Learning Outside My Field

I like getting curious about things outside economics.

History

I enjoy reading about the history of Iran and other countries. I think of what really happened in the past as a puzzle. When we read history, we get some pieces of that puzzle, but most of the pieces are still missing. We have to find the right theories and connections to reconstruct the bigger picture.

Math

Recently, I’ve been fascinated by Gödel’s incompleteness theorems. What I find especially interesting is the role that axioms play: every formal system starts with a set of assumptions, and what we can prove depends fundamentally on those assumptions. This has made me think differently about economic modeling, where we also begin by choosing a set of axioms and assumptions and then derive what follows from them. I find it fascinating to think about not only what a model can explain, but also what its assumptions make impossible for it to explain. I’m still trying to fully understand Gödel’s theorems and what they might teach me about modeling, reasoning, and the limits of what we can learn from a given set of assumptions.
