I study how agents on different sides of a market search for and match with each other, especially when there is heterogeneity on both sides. My job market paper is an example of this. I study how agents search and get matched in transportation markets, where agents are differentiated by their locations, and where location affects their ability to compete for different markets. I develop a search and matching model in which carriers and travelers are differentiated by location, and study how these differences shape competition, market access, and the matches that form.

Job Market Paper

The Role of the Remote Matching in Transport Markets

I study how carriers and customers search for and match with each other in transportation markets. Carriers are differentiated by their current location and customers by their origin–destination pair. Because customers contract before carriers arrive, a customer can match either with a carrier already nearby or with a remote carrier that must travel to reach it. Matching remotely draws on a larger pool of carriers and raises the chance of a match, but the customer must wait while the carrier travels. I develop a dynamic spatial directed search model in which customers choose whether to attract nearby or distant carriers, and carriers choose which markets to compete for given how far they are from each one. The model delivers a price for each market and shows how market conditions determine whether customers or carriers bear the cost of the pickup trip. Because a carrier’s location determines which customers it can compete for, remote matching links markets across locations, and conditions in one market spread to others through the carriers they share.
