The Economics of Chokepoints: Remote Matching and Entry Deterrence in Global Oil Tanker Markets

Navid Safahieh

This paper argues they also act as an entry cost: because tankers are chartered remotely and days in advance, an anticipated chokepoint restriction deters carrier entry before any physical closure occurs.

  • CV (PDF)

Maritime chokepoints are conventionally modeled as solely an increase in the marginal cost of transport: closing a strait makes voyages longer, so the fare rises. This paper argues that chokepoints also change who participates in which market, acting as an entry cost and not only a transport cost.

The mechanism turns on how tankers are actually chartered. Vessels are fixed remotely and in advance — days before they reach a loading port, and often from the other side of the world — so a carrier is not matched with whatever cargo happens to be nearby, but chooses which distant market to commit to under uncertainty about conditions on arrival. Because that commitment is forward-looking and global in scope, an anticipated chokepoint restriction deters entry before any physical closure occurs, a channel that local, random-matching frameworks cannot generate.

The framework distinguishes gateways, chokepoints whose closure disconnects a trading region, such as the Strait of Hormuz, from passages, which have viable substitutes, such as Bab el-Mandeb. Gateway disruptions shift the fleet sharply and the shift persists after reopening; passage disruptions produce gradual rerouting.

I show that carriers withdraw from gateway-affected markets strictly before closure, and that freight rates understate the cost of disruption because cargoes that attract no applicants leave no price record. The 2026 Strait of Hormuz restriction supplies the identifying episode: it includes a pre-closure state physically identical to normal operation, so any difference in carrier behavior is anticipation by construction.

Publications

  • Modeling the Accuracy of Traffic Crash Prediction Models

    IATSS Research, 2022. With Mohammad Hesam Rashidi, Soheil Keshavarz, Parham Pazari, and Amir Samimi.

    Models forecasting accuracy across 31 Iranian provinces using macroeconomic variables and road traffic indicators. Crash variability, average historical crash count, employment ratio, GDP per capita, and traffic exposure are the variables that affect forecast accuracy.

Working Papers

  • When Outside Options Shape the Labor Market: A Directed Search Perspective

    Studies how differences in outside options across workers affect wages, firm entry, and job allocation. I develop a directed search model with skilled and unskilled workers and high- and low-tech firms, where only skilled workers hold outside options. Stronger outside options raise posted wages for skilled workers but can reduce high-tech firm entry; as fewer high-tech jobs are created, skilled workers’ expected wages may fall, and unskilled workers are affected indirectly through changes in firm composition and wage competition.

  • The Heterogeneous Effects of Exogenous Shocks on Public Transportation Demand

    With Kowsar Yousefi and Amir Samimi.

    A sudden gasoline price shock increased subway usage by roughly 3.9 percent, implying a cross-price elasticity of about 0.044, while subway usage fell by roughly 42.6 percent during the COVID-19 pandemic.
